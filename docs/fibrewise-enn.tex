\begin{abstract}
We consider Bayesian optimization of expensive black-box objectives over high-dimensional
continuous, quantized, and mixed-variable domains.  Our starting point is a local structural
hypothesis: objective variation is dominated by a low-dimensional block pseudometric whose null
classes form approximately invariant fibres.  We introduce quotient-robust epistemic
nearest-neighbour upper confidence bound (QR-ENN-UCB), which maintains a confidence set of plausible
pseudometrics and optimizes an upper confidence bound jointly over candidate points and geometries.
Each round evaluates one objective value; coordinate perturbations are used only to construct the
finite acquisition domain.  We establish an additive simple-regret cost for approximate fibres, an
impossibility result for certifying null directions from finitely many unrestricted observations, a
finite-candidate regret decomposition into prediction uncertainty, geometry uncertainty, candidate
coverage, and quotient misspecification, and a conditional nonparametric rate governed by quotient
dimension rather than ambient dimension.  We then give a systems design for quantized model
parameters and map the resulting method onto implicit candidate descriptors, bit-packed storage,
and accelerator-resident ENN scoring.  The framework separates the statistical claim of dimension
reduction from the representation and computational mechanisms used to realize it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Bayesian optimization (BO) allocates a small budget of expensive function evaluations by combining
a surrogate model with an acquisition rule \cite{Frazier,GPUCB}.  Its application to
high-dimensional parameter spaces is limited by two distinct costs.  Statistically, nonparametric
prediction and candidate coverage deteriorate with dimension.  Computationally, conventional
surrogates and acquisition optimizers can become more expensive than the black-box interface can
tolerate.  Existing high-dimensional BO methods address these costs through local trust regions,
random or adaptive subspaces, additive structure, and specialized sampling schemes
\cite{TuRBO,REMBO,BAxUS,CylTS}.  These methods still require the optimizer to specify, assume, or
learn which directions are relevant.

We study the case in which relevance is local and may be degenerate.  Within a trust region, the
objective may vary substantially along a small number of directions while remaining nearly constant
on large sets of configurations.  A positive-definite metric can rescale directions but cannot
express exact invariance.  We instead use a pseudometric: zero-distance classes represent
configurations that the objective cannot distinguish up to a controlled error.  The induced quotient
is allowed to vary between trust regions.

Learning this geometry from the evaluations used for BO creates a model-selection problem.  A
single regularized metric estimate can assign zero weight to an untested direction and subsequently
prevent the acquisition function from testing it.  QR-ENN-UCB retains a set of geometries compatible
with the observations.  Its acquisition function is optimistic over this set, and its regret bound
contains an explicit geometry-disagreement term.  Dimension reduction is therefore claimed only
when the geometry set contracts and the fibre approximation error remains controlled.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The paper makes four methodological contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It formulates local dimension reduction as approximate factorization through an
objective-dominating pseudometric and isolates the resulting fibre-misspecification cost.
\item It separates a domination set, which preserves valid objective bounds, from an identification
set, which supports model selection and approximate elimination of directions.  A finite-sample
impossibility theorem shows why these roles cannot be conflated.
\item It introduces QR-ENN-UCB and proves a finite-candidate regret decomposition whose terms expose
the statistical requirements for ambient-dimension-independent behavior.
\item It gives an implementation design for quantized high-dimensional models that distinguishes
the logical optimization domain, coordinate proposal kernel, ENN metric, and physical encoding.
\end{enumerate}

The present results do not constitute a global no-regret theorem.  Such a theorem additionally
requires contraction of geometry uncertainty, adaptive candidate-coverage guarantees, and control
of trust-region transitions.  We state these missing conditions explicitly rather than absorbing
them into an oracle assumption.

\subsection{Problem formulation}\label{sec:problem}

Let
\[
  \X=\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\X_\ell,
  \qquad D=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}n_\ell,
\]
be a compact continuous, discrete, quantized, or mixed decision space.  At round $t$, an algorithm chooses
$x_t\in\X$ based on the filtration $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ and receives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle}
  Y_t=F(x_t)+\varepsilon_t,
  \qquad
  \E(\varepsilon_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},x_t)=0,
\end{equation}
where the noise is conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian.  We maximize $F$; minimization reverses
signs.  At each round the algorithm constructs a finite candidate set $A_t\subset\X$, evaluates an
acquisition function on $A_t$, and queries one selected point.  The candidate set may depend on the
past, subject to the measurability conditions used in the confidence analysis.

\subsection{Quantized domains and proposal kernels}\label{sec:proposal-domain}

For quantized numerical parameters, the logical search space is a Cartesian product of finite code
alphabets,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantized-domain}
  \mathcal C=\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathcal Z_\ell^{n_\ell},
  \qquad \mathcal Z_\ell=\{0,\ldots,2^{b_\ell}-1\},
  \qquad q_\ell:\mathcal Z_\ell^{n_\ell}\to\R^{n_\ell},
\end{equation}
where $q_\ell$ decodes a block using its quantization format and scale.  The numerical geometry is
defined on decoded values.  Domains combining continuous, integer, ordinal, or categorical
variables fall under mixed-variable optimization \cite{MiVaBo,Casmopolitan}.

For $c,z\in\mathcal C$, define the generalized Hamming distance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hamming-support}
  d_H(c,z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{r=1}^{n_\ell}
  \mathbf 1\{c_{\ell r}\ne z_{\ell r}\}.
\end{equation}
The support-restricted proposal kernel samples $s$ coordinate indices and changes only those
coordinates, so its support lies in the Hamming ball
$\mathcal N_s(c)=\{z:d_H(c,z)\le s\}$.  We refer to this as fixed-cardinality coordinate mutation
or a support-restricted coordinate proposal.  A second proposal kernel may perturb coordinates
across the full vector.  Hamming trust regions and local acquisition search are standard in
categorical and combinatorial BO \cite{Casmopolitan,COMBO}; the decoded metric introduced below
additionally records the numerical magnitude and objective relevance of code changes.

Both proposal kernels define finite acquisition domains; neither constructs a finite-difference
gradient.  QR-ENN-UCB predicts and acquires over the resulting candidates and evaluates one point per
round.  Section~\ref{sec:zoo-boundary} distinguishes this feedback model from zeroth-order gradient
estimation.

\section{Local quotient model}

For decoded block values $q_\ell(x)\in\R^{n_\ell}$ define
\[
\begin{aligned}
  D_\ell(x,z)&=\|q_\ell(x)-q_\ell(z)\|_2^2,\\
  G(g)&=\diag(g_1I_{n_1},\ldots,g_LI_{n_L}),\\
  m_g(x,z)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}g_\ell D_\ell(x,z),
  &d_g(x,z)&=m_g(x,z)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
for $g\in\R_+^L$.  Zero coordinates are intentional: $d_g$ is a pseudometric.  Its equivalence
classes define a quotient map
\[
  \pi_g:U\longrightarrow B_g=U/{\sim_g},
  \qquad x\sim_g z\Longleftrightarrow d_g(x,z)=0,
\]
inside a local region $U\subset\X$.  A block weight is therefore not merely a length scale.  At zero
it asserts that all allowed movement in that block is observationally invisible up to a declared
error.

\begin{definition}[admissible quotient geometry]\label{def:admissible}
For $\alpha\in(0,1]$, $L_F>0$, and $\eta\ge0$, a geometry $g$ is
$(\alpha,L_F,\eta)$-admissible for $F$ on $U$ if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domination}
  |F(x)-F(z)|\le L_F d_g(x,z)^\alpha+2\eta
  \qquad (x,z\in U).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
If $F(x)=f(\pi_g(x))+e(x)$, $f$ is $\alpha$-H\"older under $d_g$, and
$\|e\|_{\infty,U}\le\eta$, then \eqref{eq:domination} follows.  Conversely, when $d_g(x,z)=0$,
\eqref{eq:domination} bounds every fibre's objective diameter by $2\eta$.  Define the exact local
half-oscillation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
  \eta_U(g)=\frac12\sup_{b\in B_g}
  \left\{\sup_{x\in\pi_g^{-1}(b)}F(x)-
  \inf_{x\in\pi_g^{-1}(b)}F(x)\right\}.
\end{equation}

The quotient terminology has its standard mathematical meaning: a function constant on equivalence
classes factors uniquely through the corresponding quotient.  Here the equivalence classes are the
zero-distance fibres of $d_g$, and the residual in \eqref{eq:eta} quantifies approximate rather than
exact factorization.

\begin{proposition}[cost of quotienting]\label{prop:quotient-cost}
Let $f_g(b)$ be the midpoint of the range of $F$ on the fibre over $b$.  For any queried lifts
$x_t\in\pi_g^{-1}(b_t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-regret}
 \max_{x\in U}F(x)-\max_{t\le T}F(x_t)
 \le
 \max_{b\in B_g}f_g(b)-\max_{t\le T}f_g(b_t)+2\eta_U(g).
\end{equation}
The constant $2$ is sharp when the lifts are uncontrolled.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By construction, $|F(x)-f_g(\pi_g(x))|\le\eta_U(g)$.  Apply opposite sides of this inequality
to an optimizer and the best queried lift.  Opposite fibre endpoints attain equality.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-cost} isolates the approximation cost of dimension reduction: an
approximate quotient exchanges ambient dimension for a simple-regret floor.  Consequently,
$\eta_U(g)$ must appear in any guarantee based on an inexact quotient.

\subsection{Proposal geometry versus quotient geometry}

Sampling on an ellipsoid or hypersphere changes the proposal measure and may improve radial
concentration or candidate coverage; cylindrical sampling uses this effect in high-dimensional BO
\cite{CylTS}.  Such a proposal does not by itself reduce the number of directions along which $F$
varies.  QR-ENN-UCB samples metric balls or spheres under each plausible geometry, while dimensional
reduction arises only from the null classes of the pseudometric.

\section{Identifiability of quotient geometry}

A natural baseline chooses the minimum-complexity metric that majorizes observed response differences.
For local pairs $E_t$, one might solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-lp}
\begin{aligned}
  (\widehat g_t,\widehat\xi_t)\in\argmin_{g,\xi\ge0}\quad&
  c^\top g+C\sum_{(i,j)\in E_t}\omega_{ij}\xi_{ij},\\
  \text{subject to}\quad&
  h_{ij}\le \langle g,D_{ij}\rangle+\xi_{ij},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $D_{ij}=(D_1(x_i,x_j),\ldots,D_L(x_i,x_j))$ and $h_{ij}$ is a
noise-corrected squared response separation.  The $\ell_1$ objective encourages zero blocks and is
computationally attractive.  It is not, by itself, a confidence procedure.  If two blocks are
correlated in the sampled configurations, the linear program can assign all variation to either one.  If a
block has not been tested, the cost term sets it to zero precisely because there is no evidence.

The failure is statistical, not a tuning defect.

\begin{theorem}[finite data cannot certify a null direction]\label{thm:no-collapse}
Let $U\subset\R^D$ be nonempty and open, let $S=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\subset U$ be finite, and let
$\ell$ be a coordinate block.  There exist smooth objectives $F_0,F_1:U\to\R$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $F_0(x_i)=F_1(x_i)$ for every $x_i\in S$;
\item block $\ell$ is null for $F_0$;
\item block $\ell$ is non-null for $F_1$ and the two objectives have different maxima.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, under the same observation-noise law, no rule based only on observations at $S$ can
distinguish the two objectives or certify that block $\ell$ is globally null.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a point $z$ and a small ball $V\subset U$ disjoint from $S$.  Let $\varphi$ be a smooth bump
supported in $V$ whose value changes in block $\ell$.  Take $F_0=0$ and $F_1=\varphi$.  They agree
on $S$, while only $F_1$ varies in block $\ell$ and has a positive maximum.  Since their response
distributions on $S$ coincide, every observation-measurable decision has the same law under both.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[requirements for exact elimination]
An exact zero requires additional structure: known invariance, exhaustive coverage at the relevant
resolution, an equivalence test with a declared smoothness class and power, or an explicit prior-risk
assumption.  In the absence of such structure, a strictly positive reference geometry preserves
reachability and small weights represent approximate local irrelevance rather than exact invariance.
\end{remark}

\section{Confidence sets for quotient geometry}

We use a set-valued geometry estimate and separate evidence that guarantees objective domination
from evidence that identifies a parsimonious geometry.

\subsection{Domination set}

Suppose a simultaneous event provides bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noise-envelope}
  |Y_i-F(x_i)|\le b_i
  \qquad (i<t).
\end{equation}
For a proposed $(\alpha,L_F,\eta)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secant-threshold}
  a_{ij}=
  \left(
    \frac{(|Y_i-Y_j|-b_i-b_j-2\eta)_+}{L_F}
  \right)^{2/\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Let $\Gamma=[0,\bar g_1]\times\cdots\times[0,\bar g_L]$ encode physically meaningful maximum
scales and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dom-set}
  \Gg_t^{\rm dom}=
  \left\{g\in\Gamma:
  a_{ij}\le\langle g,D_{ij}\rangle
  \text{ for all }(i,j)\in E_t\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is a polytope.  Scale is retained in $g$ for statistical domination; candidate motion can use
a separately normalized copy.

\begin{proposition}[coverage of the true geometry]\label{prop:dom-coverage}
On event \eqref{eq:noise-envelope}, if $g_\star\in\Gamma$ is
$(\alpha,L_F,\eta)$-admissible on $U$, then $g_\star\in\Gg_t^{\rm dom}$ for every $t$ whose pair
set lies in $U$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The reverse triangle inequality and \eqref{eq:noise-envelope} give
$(|Y_i-Y_j|-b_i-b_j-2\eta)_+\le
(|F(x_i)-F(x_j)|-2\eta)_+$.  Apply \eqref{eq:domination}, raise both sides to $2/\alpha$, and use
$d_{g_\star}^2=\langle g_\star,D_{ij}\rangle$.
\end{proof}

The proposition survives adaptive BO locations because it only needs a simultaneous noise event;
time-uniform sub-Gaussian confidence sequences or a summable allocation of failure probabilities can
provide such an event.  But $\Gg_t^{\rm dom}$ has a crucial limitation: its constraints are lower
bounds on weighted distances.  Increasing a coordinate of $g$ preserves feasibility.  Domination
data can prove that a block is needed, but cannot prove that a block is unnecessary.

\subsection{Identification set}

Let $\Ii_t\subseteq\Gamma$ be a confidence set from a predeclared identification model, and require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:id-coverage}
  \Pp(g_\star\in\Ii_t\text{ for every }t)\ge1-\delta_{\rm geom}.
\end{equation}
The active geometry set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:active-set}
  \Gg_t=\Gg_t^{\rm dom}\cap\Ii_t.
\end{equation}
If this intersection is empty, at least one of $L_F$, $\eta$, the noise envelope, or the
identification model must be relaxed.  Projection to a metric with unsupported zero block weights
would invalidate the coverage event.

We consider three constructions for $\Ii_t$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Finite model family.}  Predeclare geometries
$g^{(1)},\ldots,g^{(J)}$, run residual tests, and eliminate a model only after a time-uniform test
rejects its advertised domination and calibration guarantees.  This construction is the most direct
and is closest to bandit model selection \cite{DynamicBalancing}.
\item \textbf{Local variogram model.}  Use randomized, predictable block perturbations to estimate
the blockwise second moment of objective increments.  Confidence intervals for those moments induce
a box or ellipsoid for $g$.  This can identify small directions, but its conclusion is local and
requires curvature control.
\item \textbf{Structural invariance.}  A decoder or simulator may declare transformations known to
leave the evaluated system's behaviour unchanged.  These directions can be quotiented exactly without reward
evidence.
\end{enumerate}

The point estimate \eqref{eq:point-lp} remains useful as one representative of $\Gg_t$, not as the
whole state.  Additional representatives include coordinate extremizers
\[
  g_{t,\ell}^{\min}\in\argmin_{g\in\Gg_t}g_\ell,
  \qquad
  g_{t,\ell}^{\max}\in\argmax_{g\in\Gg_t}g_\ell,
\]
and geometries returned by acquisition maximization.  Their disagreement is the uncertainty that
prevents self-confirming collapse.

\subsection{Local identification through block variograms}

The following calculation explains why blockwise perturbations are informative without turning the
optimizer into a finite-difference method.

\begin{lemma}[block variogram]\label{lem:variogram}
Suppose $F$ is $C^2$ near $c$ with $\|\nabla^2F\|_{\rm op}\le H$.  Let
$\Delta=(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_L)$ have independent centred blocks with
$\|\Delta_\ell\|=r_\ell$ and
$\E\Delta_\ell\Delta_\ell^\top=(r_\ell^2/n_\ell)I$.  As
$r=(\sum_\ell r_\ell^2)^{1/2}\to0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variogram}
  \E\{F(c+\Delta)-F(c)\}^2
  =\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}
  \frac{\|\nabla_\ell F(c)\|_2^2}{n_\ell}r_\ell^2
  +O(\|\nabla F(c)\|Hr^3+H^2r^4).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Taylor-expand at $c$.  Centring and block independence remove cross terms, and isotropy gives the
displayed quadratic term.  The first-order term is $O(\|\nabla F(c)\|r)$ and the Hessian remainder
is $O(Hr^2)$, so their cross term and the squared remainder give the stated error.
\end{proof}

Thus a natural infinitesimal target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinitesimal-g}
  g_\ell^\circ(c)=\frac{\|\nabla_\ell F(c)\|_2^2}{n_\ell}.
\end{equation}
Support-restricted coordinate proposals can estimate \eqref{eq:variogram} over many rounds by comparing a new
reward to a stored centre or local anchor.  The selected candidate evaluation still contributes to optimization;
there is no mandatory antithetic mate.  With noisy objectives, a reused noisy anchor induces
dependence and a squared-increment noise bias.  A rigorous estimator must use a replicate-averaged
anchor, independent anchor observations, or a martingale analysis that includes that dependence.

Small gradient at one point is not a fibre certificate.  If
$\|\nabla^2F\|_{\rm op}\le H$ on a radius-$\rho$ region and a confidence bound gives
$\|\nabla_\ell F(c)\|\le u_\ell$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-gradient-bound}
  \sup_{x\in U}\|\nabla_\ell F(x)\|
  \le u_\ell+H\rho.
\end{equation}
If $Z$ is a proposed set of null blocks and every two points on one proposed fibre can be joined by
a path whose total motion in block $\ell$ is at most $R_\ell$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-certificate}
  2\eta_U(g)
  \le\sum_{\ell\in Z}R_\ell(u_\ell+H\rho).
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:fibre-certificate} provides an approximate-collapse certificate: it
combines statistical uncertainty, curvature, and trust-region scale.  It also explains why a block
may become negligible only after the region contracts.

\section{ENN surrogate and calibration}

For a query $x$ and geometry $g$, retrieve the $k$ nearest prediction observations under $d_g$.
For noise estimates $s_i^2$, define inverse-distance weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:enn-weights}
  a_{i,g}(x)=\{\epsilon+s_i^2+\lambda d_g(x,x_i)^2\}^{-1},
  \qquad
  w_{i,g}(x)=\frac{a_{i,g}(x)}{\sum_j a_{j,g}(x)},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:enn-predictor}
  \widehat F_{t,g}(x)=\sum_i w_{i,g}(x)Y_i,
  \qquad
  N_{\rm eff}(t,g,x)=\frac{1}{\sum_iw_{i,g}(x)^2}.
\end{equation}
Write $r_{t,g,k}(x)=\max_{i:w_{i,g}(x)>0}d_g(x,x_i)$.

ENNX's internal epistemic and aleatoric scales are useful acquisition features.  They become a
Bayesian posterior only under a generative model, and they become frequentist confidence bounds only
after a coverage argument.  We use ``ENN-UCB'' to name the decision rule, not to assert that the
existing ENNX scale already has the following guarantee.

\begin{theorem}[cross-fitted ENN interval]\label{thm:enn-interval}
Fix $g$, a query $x$, and neighbour weights that are independent of the prediction-fold response
noises.  Suppose $g$ is $(\alpha,L_F,\eta_g)$-admissible and the selected noises are independent
$\sigma$-sub-Gaussian variables.  Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:enn-radius}
  |\widehat F_{t,g}(x)-F(x)|\le C_{t,g}(x;\delta),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:confidence-radius}
  C_{t,g}(x;\delta)=
  L_F r_{t,g,k}(x)^\alpha+2\eta_g+
  \sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta)\sum_iw_{i,g}(x)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every selected neighbour, admissibility bounds the deterministic difference from $F(x)$ by
$L_Fr_{t,g,k}(x)^\alpha+2\eta_g$.  Convex averaging preserves this bound.  The weighted noise sum
is sub-Gaussian with proxy variance $\sigma^2\sum_iw_{i,g}(x)^2$.
\end{proof}

Strict independence can be obtained with a geometry/training stream and a prediction stream whose
locations are committed before their responses are observed.  For Theorem~\ref{thm:enn-interval}, $g$, the candidate
pool, and the centre used to generate that pool must be measurable without the prediction-stream
responses.  Retrospectively splitting a single adaptive BO trajectory is insufficient: later
locations encode earlier noise.  Cross-fitting can rotate genuinely valid streams, while an all-data
predictor needs a separate predictable-weight or martingale proof.  This independence condition is
essential because reusing outcomes both to choose $g$ and to report an interval under $g$ can cause
systematic undercoverage.

Conditionally on a sigma-field under which a finite candidate pool $A_t$, a finite representative
geometry set $Q_t$, and all prediction weights are fixed independently of prediction-fold noise, a
union bound uses $\delta/(|A_t||Q_t|)$.  Finiteness alone is not enough when the set itself depends
on that noise.  A continuous geometry set requires either a uniform empirical-process bound or a
finite net plus a continuity error.  Our finite implementation uses a small $Q_t$ and reports the
resulting discretization error; continuous-set optimization is not treated as exact.

\section{Quotient-robust acquisition and regret}

Let $Q_t\subset\Gg_t$ be a finite representative set.  For each $g\in Q_t$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intervals}
  u_{t,g}(x)=\widehat F_{t,g}(x)+C_{t,g}(x),
  \qquad
  \ell_{t,g}(x)=\widehat F_{t,g}(x)-C_{t,g}(x).
\end{equation}
The quotient-robust upper envelope and computable geometry disagreement are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:robust-envelope}
  U_t(x)=\max_{g\in Q_t}u_{t,g}(x),
  \qquad
  \Omega_t(x)=
  \max_{g\in Q_t}u_{t,g}(x)-\min_{g\in Q_t}u_{t,g}(x).
\end{equation}
The acquisition rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qr-ucb}
  x_t\in\argmax_{x\in A_t}U_t(x).
\end{equation}
Only $x_t$ is evaluated.  Maximizing over $g$ implements optimism over structural uncertainty
without imposing a posterior distribution on metrics.

\begin{proposition}[one-step robust regret]\label{prop:robust-regret}
Let $x_U^\star\in\argmax_{x\in U}F(x)$ and
$x_{A_t}^\star\in\argmax_{x\in A_t}F(x)$.  Define the candidate-pool gap
\[
  \Delta_t^{\rm pool}=F(x_U^\star)-F(x_{A_t}^\star).
\]
Suppose $g_\star\in Q_t$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:enn-interval}'s interval holds for all
$x\in A_t$ under $g_\star$.  Then the selection \eqref{eq:qr-ucb} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:robust-regret-bound}
  F(x_U^\star)-F(x_t)
  \le \Delta_t^{\rm pool}+2C_{t,g_\star}(x_t)+\Gamma_t(x_t)
  \le \Delta_t^{\rm pool}+2C_{t,g_\star}(x_t)+\Omega_t(x_t),
\end{equation}
where
\[
  \Gamma_t(x)=U_t(x)-u_{t,g_\star}(x).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At the pool optimizer,
$F(x_{A_t}^\star)\le u_{t,g_\star}(x_{A_t}^\star)\le U_t(x_{A_t}^\star)$.
Selection gives $U_t(x_{A_t}^\star)\le U_t(x_t)$, while the true-geometry lower interval gives
$F(x_t)\ge\ell_{t,g_\star}(x_t)$.  Hence
\[
 F(x_{A_t}^\star)-F(x_t)
 \le U_t(x_t)-\ell_{t,g_\star}(x_t)
 =\Gamma_t(x_t)+2C_{t,g_\star}(x_t).
\]
Add the pool gap.  Since $u_{t,g_\star}$ is one member of the finite family,
$\Gamma_t\le\Omega_t$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:robust-regret} gives the central decomposition.  A point estimate suppresses
$\Omega_t$ operationally without eliminating geometry uncertainty statistically.  Robust
acquisition retains this term and allocates evaluations where plausible geometries disagree.  If
the geometries make similar predictions at selected points, $\Omega_t$ is small even when their raw
weights differ; prediction disagreement is therefore more relevant than parameter distance.

If $g_\star\notin Q_t$ but $Q_t$ is an $\varepsilon_t$-net of a confidence set containing it, and
$u_{t,g}(x)$ is $K_t$-Lipschitz in $g$ on that set, the same argument adds at most
$2K_t\varepsilon_t$.  This is the geometry-discretization term.  Near zero weights, neighbour
identities can change discontinuously, so $K_t$ may be large.  We therefore include coordinate
extremes and observed neighbour-order breakpoints in the representative set.

Summing \eqref{eq:robust-regret-bound} gives the conditional cumulative-regret statement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cumulative}
  R_T
  \le \sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_t^{\rm pool}
  +2\sum_{t=1}^{T}C_{t,g_\star}(x_t)
  +\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Omega_t(x_t),
\end{equation}
up to geometry discretization and trust-region transition terms.  A sublinear theorem therefore
requires all three sums to be controlled.  Geometry coverage alone is not enough.

\subsection{Intrinsic-dimension dependence}

Let $q$ denote a local covering dimension under the true pseudometric, not the count of nonzero
ambient coordinates.  Suppose that, conditionally on the selected region, the prediction history is
quasi-uniform enough that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:knn-radius-rate}
  r_{t,g_\star,k}(x_t)\le C_r(k/t)^{1/q}
\end{equation}
and the normalized weights satisfy
$\sum_iw_{i,g_\star}(x_t)^2\le C_w/k$.  These are substantive sampling assumptions; adaptive BO
does not grant them for free.

\begin{corollary}[conditional quotient rate]\label{cor:quotient-rate}
Under \eqref{eq:knn-radius-rate}, exact fibres $\eta_{g_\star}=0$, bounded logarithmic factors, and
$k_t\asymp t^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+q)}$, the true-geometry ENN radius obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius-rate}
  C_{t,g_\star}(x_t)=\widetilde O
  \left(t^{-\alpha/(2\alpha+q)}\right),
\end{equation}
and its contribution to \eqref{eq:cumulative} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cumulative-rate}
  \widetilde O
  \left(T^{1-\alpha/(2\alpha+q)}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The H\"older bias is $O((k/t)^{\alpha/q})$ and the stochastic term is
$\widetilde O(k^{-1/2})$.  Balancing them gives the stated $k_t$ and radius.  Sum the resulting
power law.
\end{proof}

Ambient dimension $D$ has disappeared from this conditional statistical term, but it can re-enter
through candidate generation, geometry identification, $\Omega_t$, or a poor block family.  This
is the precise sense in which quotient BO could scale, and also the list of ways it can fail.

\section{Candidate-set construction and coverage}

For a centre $c$, metric radius $\rho$, and coordinate-change budget $s$, let
$d_{\rm supp}(x,c)$ count the coordinates whose values differ.  On the quantization-code domain
$\mathcal C$, this is $d_H$ from \eqref{eq:hamming-support}; in a continuous decoded chart it is
$\|q(x)-q(c)\|_0$.  Define the intersection of a pseudometric ball and a support ball,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trust-region}
  \Tt_g(c,\rho,s)=
  \{x\in\X:d_g(x,c)\le\rho,
  d_{\rm supp}(x,c)\le s\}.
\end{equation}
The support ball controls how many variables change, while $d_g$ controls the decoded magnitude and
objective-relevant geometry of those changes.  A continuous candidate can be formed by drawing a
support-restricted direction $z$ on a unit sphere or in a unit ball and applying a scaled lift
$A_gz$ satisfying $A_g^\top G(g)A_g\approx I$.  In the quantized backend, the analogue is bounded coordinate
mutation inside $\mathcal N_s(c)$, followed by decoding for metric evaluation.  For zero or tiny
weights, step sizes must be capped by block radii and a reference metric; otherwise an inverse square
root would produce unbounded coordinate changes.

The robust pool is a union
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pool-union}
  A_t=A_t^{\rm ref}\cup
  \bigcup_{g\in Q_t}A_{t,g},
  \qquad A_{t,g}\subset\Tt_g(c_t,\rho_t,s_t).
\end{equation}
Candidate descriptors can share seeds across geometries so that differences are attributable to
scaling rather than Monte Carlo noise.  $A_t^{\rm ref}$ uses a fixed strictly positive reference geometry and
prevents an incorrect learned family from making part of the decision space unreachable.

The pool term has a simple dimension-dependent interpretation.  Suppose the candidate law under
$g_\star$ assigns mass at least $c\epsilon^{q/\alpha}$ to the $\epsilon$-optimal set in the current
region.  For $M$ independent true-geometry candidates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pool-probability}
  \Pp(\Delta_t^{\rm pool}>\epsilon)
  \le \exp(-Mc\epsilon^{q/\alpha}).
\end{equation}
Thus, with probability $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pool-rate}
  \Delta_t^{\rm pool}
  \le
  \left(\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{cM}\right)^{\alpha/q}.
\end{equation}
This elementary bound explains why large cheap candidate pools matter.  It also explains why a
sphere alone is insufficient: the exponent improves only when the proposal reaches a
$q$-dimensional near-optimal set under a valid geometry.

\section{QR-ENN-UCB algorithm}

We call the proposed method \textbf{QR-ENN-UCB}: quotient-robust epistemic nearest-neighbour upper
confidence bound.  The analysis uses independent geometry/training and prediction streams as
specified in Theorem~\ref{thm:enn-interval}.  An all-data implementation is a practical
approximation whose adaptive calibration is evaluated empirically in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\begin{algorithm}[QR-ENN-UCB]
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Localize.}  Choose the active region $U_t$, centre $c_t$, metric radius $\rho_t$,
and coordinate-change budget $s_t$ from trust-region state.
\item \textbf{Update evidence.}  Add valid secant constraints to $\Gg_t^{\rm dom}$ and update the
predeclared identification set $\Ii_t$.  If their intersection is empty, relax the structural model
or restart the local region.
\item \textbf{Represent geometry.}  Build $Q_t$ from the point-LP solution, the reference metric,
coordinate extremizers, surviving finite models, and any geometry that maximized the previous
acquisition.  Cap $|Q_t|$ by merging metrics with indistinguishable predictions.
\item \textbf{Generate candidates.}  Using shared seeds, sample bounded coordinate proposals inside
each $\Tt_g(c_t,\rho_t,s_t)$ and add reference-geometry and restart candidates.
\item \textbf{Predict.}  Run ENN neighbour retrieval and prediction for every $(x,g)\in A_t\times
Q_t$.  Form calibrated $u_{t,g}(x)$ and the robust envelope $U_t(x)$.
\item \textbf{Acquire.}  Select $x_t\in\argmax_{x\in A_t,g\in Q_t}u_{t,g}(x)$.  Break ties by
larger geometry disagreement, then by a deterministic seed order.
\item \textbf{Evaluate once.}  Query $Y_t$, append the observation with its preassigned fold, and
update success, coverage, geometry-disagreement, and region-transition diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

We record the acquisition-maximizing geometry alongside the selected configuration.  If a candidate
geometry repeatedly selects configurations whose rewards violate its intervals or whose empirical regret
exceeds its candidate bound, it becomes eligible for elimination.  Balancing or elimination methods
for bandit model selection provide a template for this logic \cite{DynamicBalancing}; directly
importing their regret theorem would still require each geometry-specific ENN learner to satisfy its
advertised bound.

\subsection{Trust-region updates}

TuRBO supplies a practical success/failure mechanism for local BO regions \cite{TuRBO}.  We expand
a region only when all of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item incumbent improvement is statistically credible;
\item held-out ENN coverage is not below target;
\item $\Omega_t$ is small near the proposed expanded boundary;
\item the fibre certificate \eqref{eq:fibre-certificate} remains below its budget.
\end{enumerate}
The region contracts when local coverage fails or curvature invalidates the geometry, and restarts when
no member of $\Gg_t$ is admissible at a useful $\eta$.  These tests distinguish objective failure
from surrogate failure and geometry failure.

Different regions may have different quotients.  On an overlap, two metrics $g_a,g_b$ are
compatible at distortion $\kappa$ if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transition}
  \kappa^{-1}d_{g_a}(x,z)\le d_{g_b}(x,z)
  \le\kappa d_{g_a}(x,z)
\end{equation}
on held-out secants.  Distortion multiplies along a chain of regions; large cycle distortion is
evidence that no single global geometry is justified.  The method then maintains an atlas of local
quotients rather than averaging incompatible weights.

\section{Systems design for ENNX}

The ENNX \texttt{PackedSearch} backend realizes the quantized domain
\eqref{eq:quantized-domain}.  Each logical variable block is stored as a contiguous sequence of
4- or 8-bit quantization codes together with a decoder scale, distance weight, and proposal radius;
the current API names this record a \texttt{Leaf}.  ENN distances are accumulated from decoded
numerical differences and are therefore distinct from both bitwise Hamming distance and the
code-coordinate distance $d_H$ in \eqref{eq:hamming-support}.

Candidate generation is deterministic conditional on a centre, random seed, block metadata, and
proposal length.  The tuple of these values is an implicit candidate descriptor.  In the lazy path,
the selected descriptor is materialized as a full bit-packed vector only after acquisition.  This
reduces candidate-write traffic but does not alter the logical search space, proposal distribution,
or statistical model.

Computational viability requires geometry uncertainty not to erase the throughput advantage of
bit-packed low-precision data and accelerator-resident scoring.  The current ENNX architecture
provides the required outer loop: seed-defined candidate descriptors, resident bit-packed
observations, decoded distance/top-$k$ kernels, ENN prediction, UCB or Thompson acquisition, and
trust-region state.  The new cost is evaluating a small family of blockwise distance-weight vectors
rather than one vector.

Let $J=|Q_t|$, $M=|A_t|$, $N$ be resident history size, $k$ the retained neighbour count, and $s$
the coordinate-change budget.  The complexity depends on the proposal operator.  For a
support-restricted proposal, after the base-to-observation distances have been cached for each
geometry, each candidate distance is a rank-$s$ coordinate update.  A direct exact scorer then costs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-cost}
  O(JMN(s+k)).
\end{equation}
For the regular full-vector proposal, distance scoring examines all $D$ decoded coordinates and the
corresponding bound is $O(JMN(D+k))$.  Lazy materialization avoids writing all $D$ output codes for
unselected candidates, but does not remove the $D$ work needed to score a full-vector proposal.
Therefore a dimension-independent systems claim is valid only for the support-restricted path (or
for a future index with a proved exact-recall shortcut), not for \texttt{PackedSearch} in general.

The representative count $J$ is controlled by three mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item share seeds, selected coordinate sets, and decoded coordinate changes across geometries;
\item retain only prediction-distinct extreme geometries, not a dense parameter grid;
\item use column generation: score the current $Q_t$, then solve for a new geometry only at the few
candidates whose robust acquisition can still improve.
\end{enumerate}
An indexed shortlist replaces $N$ by shortlist size, provided the shortlist has a reference-geometry
recall guarantee.  Candidate-value quantization and any future low-precision representation of
geometry weights introduce different numerical errors and must be accounted for separately.

\subsection{Incremental implementation}

We separate the proposed implementation into independently testable components.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prediction surface.}  The \texttt{PackedSearch} ask/tell interface remains unchanged,
while the selected candidate's ENN mean and error components are exposed for calibration.
\item \textbf{Finite geometry ensemble.}  The ensemble interface accepts $J$ blockwise
distance-weight and proposal-radius vectors, generates candidates with shared seeds, scores their
Cartesian product, and returns the maximizing configuration, geometry index, mean, uncertainty, and
$\Omega_t$.
\item \textbf{Reference coverage.}  A configurable fraction of the candidate budget uses a fixed
strictly positive reference geometry.  UCB still selects the single objective query.
\item \textbf{Geometry updater.}  The CPU control plane maintains the domination polytope and
finite-model residual tests.  LP solves occur once per BO round or region update, outside the
distance kernel.
\item \textbf{Calibration.}  Preassigned folds, replay evaluation, and interval-coverage telemetry
support calibration; exact zero weights are disabled in the absence of a certificate.
\item \textbf{Continuous-set optimization.}  Coordinate extremizers and acquisition-driven column
generation extend the finite ensemble after its coverage and throughput have been established.
\end{enumerate}

The existing all-data posterior-like draw remains an empirical Thompson option.  The QR-ENN-UCB
guarantee applies only after its draws or intervals are calibrated under adaptive data.

\section{Related work}

Unknown or implicit metric bandits already exist.  Slivkins studies a taxonomy whose tree is known
but whose numerical subtree widths are hidden \cite{ImplicitMetric}.  Wanigasekara and Yu learn
nonparametric similarities among a finite set of contextual-bandit arms and adaptively partition the
context--arm space \cite{UnknownMetric}.  Input warping learns bijective transformations for
nonstationary GP BO \cite{InputWarping}, and BO with unknown stationary-kernel hyperparameters can
retain no-regret guarantees by expanding the considered function class \cite{UnknownHyper}.
Misspecified GP bandits explicitly pay for uniform approximation error \cite{MisspecifiedGP}.
These results establish that learning an unknown metric is not itself new.  QR-ENN-UCB differs by
combining a pseudometric with nontrivial null fibres, confidence sets over local quotient geometries,
an explicit geometry-disagreement term in acquisition and regret, and an ENNX-oriented systems design for
quantized high-dimensional configurations.  The same geometry controls candidate generation,
neighbour retrieval, and trust-region shape, while the fibre approximation error remains explicit.

Related high-dimensional BO methods make different assumptions.  REMBO uses a
low-dimensional embedding \cite{REMBO}; BAxUS expands nested subspaces \cite{BAxUS}; TuRBO
localizes BO \cite{TuRBO}; and cylindrical Thompson sampling changes proposal geometry
\cite{CylTS}.  QR-ENN-UCB instead tries to learn local equivalence classes from objective evidence.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:experiments}

The experimental protocol tests geometry identification, statistical calibration, optimization
performance, and systems throughput separately before evaluating the complete method.

\subsection{Synthetic benchmark families}

The synthetic family fixes quotient dimension $q$ while increasing ambient nuisance dimension $D-q$
without changing the quotient objective.  It contains five settings:
\begin{enumerate}
\item axis-aligned exact null blocks, $\eta=0$;
\item weakly active blocks with a controlled $\eta$ floor;
\item correlated blocks for which \eqref{eq:point-lp} is nonunique;
\item quotient changes across regions, requiring an atlas;
\item rotated active subspaces inside a block, which the scalar block family cannot represent.
\end{enumerate}
The fifth case is a negative control.  Scalar block weights eliminate whole blocks but cannot
recover an arbitrary low-rank subspace within a block.  Representing that setting requires the
extension $m_G(x,z)=(q(x)-q(z))^\top G(q(x)-q(z))$ with block-low-rank $G$, together with its
additional identification and systems costs.

The reported quantities are simple regret versus oracle calls and wall-clock time, coverage of
$\eta$ and ENN intervals, geometry-set coverage of $g_\star$, $\Omega_t$, effective neighbour
count, pool gap against a large offline reference pool, and candidate throughput.  Results are
stratified by $D$, $q$, $J$, noise level, and quantization format.

\subsection{Ablations}

At matched oracle-query and wall-clock budgets, we compare:
\begin{enumerate}
\item current single-geometry ENNX;
\item QR-ENN-UCB with the same point estimate but no geometry extremes;
\item robust prediction with Euclidean/reference candidate motion;
\item learned candidate motion with reference ENN distances;
\item full shared geometry;
\item TuRBO, BAxUS, cylindrical or spherical sampling, random search, and an appropriate direct-search
baseline.
\end{enumerate}
The two mismatch ablations isolate whether gains arise from better candidate
coverage, better ENN neighbourhoods, or their coupling.

\subsection{Evaluation criteria}

Evidence against the proposed scaling mechanism consists of any persistent observation below:
\begin{enumerate}
\item empirical ENN intervals undercover materially under adaptive search;
\item $\Gg_t$ loses the planted geometry more often than its declared failure probability;
\item $\Omega_t$ remains linear in the regret accounting because geometries cannot be identified;
\item nuisance dimensions force $J$, pool size, or reference exploration to scale with $D$;
\item the quotient metric improves prediction but harms candidate coverage, or conversely;
\item ensemble scoring removes the measured speed advantage over a GP or random-search baseline;
\item exact zero-weight selection improves benchmarks only because the benchmark's null directions are known in
advance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and open problems}

Proposition~\ref{prop:robust-regret} is a correct finite-pool decision statement, but it is not yet a
global no-regret theorem.  A complete theory needs the following pieces.

\paragraph{Adaptive ENN concentration.}
A time-uniform result requires either confidence sequences for predictable local weights or a fold
construction with exact independence.  Nearest-neighbour sets selected after observing an adaptive
trajectory are not automatically covered by a fixed-query concentration bound.

\paragraph{Geometry contraction.}
Sublinear cumulative regret requires assumptions under which $\Omega_t(x_t)$ is summable or
sublinear; secant domination alone is insufficient.  A promising route is a finite nested family of
quotient models with time-uniform misspecification tests followed by regret-bound balancing
\cite{DynamicBalancing,ModelSelection}.  A continuous block metric may follow by covering the
parameter set, but neighbour-order discontinuities must be handled.

\paragraph{Candidate coverage under adaptive geometry.}
Extending \eqref{eq:pool-rate} to support-restricted proposals on quantized variables requires a
coverage result for candidates generated under a mixture of learned metrics.  The relevant dimension
is likely a near-optimality or zooming dimension
\cite{XArmed}, not merely the number of active coordinates or nonzero block weights.

\paragraph{Misspecification adaptation.}
When $\eta$ is unknown, a grid of misspecification budgets must balance invalid optimism at small
$\eta$ against vacuous bounds at large $\eta$.  Phased elimination or regret balancing, analogous
to misspecified kernel bandits \cite{MisspecifiedGP}, is a plausible mechanism.

\paragraph{Local-to-global accounting.}
A global result must account for trust-region restarts and metric transitions.  A region graph with
controlled overlap distortion may support an atlas theorem; arbitrary changing quotients will not.

\paragraph{Computational geometry.}
The number of extreme metrics required to approximate
$\max_{g\in\Gg_t}u_{t,g}(x)$ after top-$k$ neighbour identities are considered is not yet
characterized.  The score is piecewise smooth over cells of metric space induced by
pairwise distance-order hyperplanes.  An
acquisition-driven traversal of only occupied cells may be much cheaper than a uniform metric net.

\section{Relation to zeroth-order gradient estimation}\label{sec:zoo-boundary}

The oracle in \eqref{eq:oracle} is zeroth order, but QR-ENN-UCB is not a zeroth-order gradient
algorithm.  If a separate method draws $u$ on a $q$-sphere and evaluates an antithetic pair at radius
$\mu$, the classical two-point estimator scales with $q$ rather than $D$ only when the quotient is
valid.  Under a residual $|e|\le\eta$, its gradient bias contains a term of order $q\eta/\mu$.
That creates a different optimization tradeoff and consumes two evaluations per direction
\cite{Duchi,Shamir}.  Such estimators may provide diagnostics, but they are outside the BO algorithm
and regret analysis developed here.

\section{Conclusion}

We introduced a pure BO framework over two coupled unknowns: the maximizing configuration and the
local geometry through which objective values can be predicted.  ENN supplies the nonparametric
surrogate, the quotient hypothesis provides a route to intrinsic-dimension dependence, and the
geometry confidence set prevents unsupported elimination of directions.  The resulting regret
decomposition is
\begin{align*}
  \text{regret}\ \lesssim\;&
  \text{pool coverage}+\text{ENN uncertainty in dimension }q\\
  &+\text{geometry disagreement}+\text{fibre misspecification}
  +\text{region transitions}.
\end{align*}
The method scales only when these terms are simultaneously estimable and the geometry-disagreement
term contracts without ambient-dimensional probing.  The proposed ENNX design makes this
condition empirically testable while retaining low-precision candidate-scoring throughput.
