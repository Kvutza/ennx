# History at billion dimensions

Source checked: 2026-09-29. Replay below is a design proposal, not implemented.

## Memory is the constraint

The active model has 1,038,508,544 FP16 coordinates.

| Payload | Bytes | GiB |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| One weight row | 2,077,017,088 | 1.934 |
| Two historical rows | 4,154,034,176 | 3.869 |
| 128 historical rows | 265,858,187,264 | 247.600 |
| 128-by-128 FP32 distance matrix | 65,536 | 0.000061 |

These are payloads, not total process memory. The incumbent, pending proposal,
scorer workspace, metadata, and OS need additional memory. Full resident history
cannot fit the 24 GB machine.

The small matrix can preserve known pairwise distances. It cannot determine
an arbitrary new candidate's distances without additional geometric information.
Near-orthogonality is an approximation argument, not an exact reconstruction rule.

## What production actually keeps

[SearchState](../rust/crates/ennx/src/bf16_metal.rs) holds two full historical
rows plus logical outcomes, variances, identities, and a bounded distance matrix.
The [selector](../rust/crates/ennx/src/bf16_search.metal) estimates a nonresident
distance by adding stored base-to-history distance and squared realized proposal
displacement. It omits the directional cross term. Stored base distances can
already contain approximation error from previous insertions.

Resident rows receive direct coordinate-wise corrections. The resulting
selected-candidate distances are appended to the fitting matrix, so both
prediction and LOOCV fitting can depend on approximate geometry.
At capacity 128, history resets to the incumbent; it is not a perpetual rolling
128-point exact archive.

The [audit](bo-audit.md) demonstrates ranking changes on synthetic fixtures,
often with very small score gaps. It does not establish corresponding training
harm or a billion-dimensional failure rate.

## Storage alternative: exact seeded replay

These vectors are generated by a known program, not arbitrary externally supplied
arrays. A compact recipe can therefore describe them without changing their
full-coordinate search space:

- an exact checkpoint, or reproducible initial weights;
- every accepted update needed to reach a historical base;
- each observation's base ID, seed/stream, radius, noise law, tensor manifest
  and fixed scales, dtype/rounding, and generator implementation identity;
- stable observation identity, reward, variance, and dependency lifetime.

For a tile of coordinates, reconstruct the relevant accepted-base lineage,
rounding to FP16 after every accepted update. Rejected observations branch from
their recorded base but do not change that lineage. Generate historical proposal
tiles and reduce distances against new candidate tiles without retaining whole
historical rows. Grouping observations by base permits sharing lineage replay.

This can bound scratch memory by tile size and history batch size, plus one
checkpoint/current-model state. It does not make computation independent of
dimension or history. Four candidates against 128 histories still require
hundreds of billions of coordinate comparisons in a direct method, plus RNG,
rounding, and lineage replay. Periodic checkpoints bound replay depth but cost
roughly 1.934 GiB per full checkpoint. Their storage tier and I/O must be measured.

Do not replace sequential rounded updates with a sum of seeded ideal directions:
FP16 rounding is not distributive. Replaying from the current incumbent by
subtracting old deltas is not generally invertible either. A seed without its
base lineage is insufficient. Same-device bitwise replay must be demonstrated;
cross-device Gaussian math and compiler changes need separate qualification.

Current selected-row replay and per-round artifacts do not constitute this
complete historical replay engine.

## Options and decision

| Approach | Geometry | Cost/trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two resident rows plus current implicit history | Exact resident, approximate nonresident | Implemented; fast bounded residency, ranking errors possible |
| Checkpoint plus seeded tiled replay | Exact realized geometry if replay passes | Small history metadata; potentially expensive regeneration |
| More resident full rows | Exact to those rows | About 1.934 GiB extra per row |
| Disk-backed full rows | Exact stored values | About 247.6 GiB for 128 rows; streaming latency, not just storage |
| Projection/sketch | Approximate | Changes the metric and requires explicit research-scope approval |

Recommended next implementation experiment: a tiled replay oracle, not 128
resident allocations. Validate it against archived full rows on small fixtures,
then measure full-model regeneration and distance time. Compare acquisition
rankings and refit on exact distances, not just query-distance substitution.

A later fast/slow hybrid could retain 128 approximate observations and selectively
replay promising or ambiguous cases. Exact reranking of a shortlist is not an
exact global winner guarantee unless exclusions have valid bounds. Such a hybrid
must remain labeled approximate and must be tested against random selection
under matched evaluation and wall-time budgets.

Voronoi-style boundary generation needs directional geometry. The present
nonresident distance approximation cannot simply be treated as a boundary oracle.
A boundary defined by two resident sites is exact only relative to that subset,
and FP16 rounding still requires realized-distance verification.
